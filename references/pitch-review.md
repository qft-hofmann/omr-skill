# Source-to-ABC pitch audit

A transcription can have correct bar durations and perfectly round-trip through MusicXML and still contain the same wrong pitches. Review the source itself; treat exported-score comparisons as a separate conversion check.

## Review procedure

- Preserve a page/system/measure map and review the whole score, including printed pickup and shortened final bars. Make a source crop and aligned proof crop visible together. Give the reviewer the actual clef, key and accidental context, including preceding notes when the crop does not show it. Do not draw target markers over clefs, key signatures, accidentals, noteheads or ledger lines.
- Compare all noteheads by staff and attack: include each chord tone, simultaneous voices, tied notes, omissions and extras. Record written spelling and octave, then distinguish the sounding octave. A guitar treble-8 clef changes sounding pitch; do not apply a second octave change merely because a MusicXML implementation already stores sounding pitches.
- For high/low ledger notes, dense chords, accidentals, damaged scans and reviewer disagreements, enlarge the scan and measure the local staff lines and the **notehead center**, not the whole stem-and-head glyph. Preserve the unrounded location, source crop and coordinate transform. If head or staff detection is uncertain, manually identify or inspect the locations and say so; no automatic pitch result can recover a missed or spurious notehead by itself.
- At the note's horizontal position, let the bottom staff line have image coordinate `yb`, the local adjacent-line spacing be `d`, and the head center be `yh`, with y increasing downward. The unrounded number of diatonic half-line steps above the bottom line is `q = 2 * (yb - yh) / d`. Round only after considering localization error. In treble clef q=0 is written E4; each step moves one diatonic note. Ledger lines continue the same spacing. Skew or curvature calls for line positions at the same x as the note, not a single page-wide baseline.
- Apply the clef, key signature, explicit and carried accidentals, octave marks and transposition separately from position arithmetic. Check the note-to-stem and accidental-to-note attachments, and preserve enharmonic spelling. Position alone does not recognize accidentals or identify a voice.
- On disagreement, return to the source, inspect the larger context and derive the reading afresh. Record verified, uncertain and unreviewed regions. Expose a concise source-linked list of residual questions; do not silently overwrite a user-checked pitch. If the crop cannot settle a note, retain uncertainty rather than count consensus as proof.
- Check pitch/rhythm and notation/text coverage separately. A compact row such as `p1/s2/mm5–8: pitches/rhythm reviewed; notation/text uncertain: m7 fingering attachment` is sufficient. Review all source notes and all candidate notes, so omissions and extras both receive attention; shared noteheads may belong to more than one musical voice. Do not equate visible-notehead count with event count.
- Review source beam levels/hooks, ties versus slurs, rests, articulations, fingerings, lyrics and directions as well as pitches. Music that sounds the same can have different source notation. A missing accidental glyph can leave playback unchanged while making the proof unfaithful.
- A source-only reading is procedurally separate from candidate comparison, but does not imply statistically independent errors. A manually located notehead plus arithmetic is additional evidence only to the extent that its geometry is correctly identified. Use objective risk and discrepancies, not self-reported confidence alone, to select closer views.
- When a correction changes key/clef/accidental context, voice assignment or a connection across a boundary, revisit the dependent passage. After correcting ABC or consumed annotations, regenerate the proof before marking that region reviewed again.

## Evidence and limitations

A source/proof comparison and a fresh source-only reading provide different kinds of review. Neither is assumed superior. Small internal experiments with provisional references do not establish equivalence or a reliability guarantee, and seeded-error detection does not measure full first-pass transcription with adaptive tools. No numerical accuracy claim is made for this skill.

For a workflow evaluation, hide reference ABC/XML from the transcriber, allow normal adaptive views/tools, and preserve the first draft and reviewed output. Evaluate residual source-reading errors and notation preservation separately from conversion failures. Record review/correction effort and unresolved cases. Include real first-pass errors and clean controls, not only seeded pitch changes; distinguish tests on original scans from clean renderings generated from reference files.
